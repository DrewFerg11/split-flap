The rail on the right of the rows

Bus wires up the side for power, one data cable, and one press-on socket per row that the row's existing header slides into. Not to scale. Drawn from rail.md (2026-10-31); nothing here is decided yet.

1 · The whole rail

Two thick wires carry 12 V and GND up the right side from the PSU. Each row taps them with two short wires, the 12 V one through its own fuse. The data cable (A, B and a spare for ENUM) runs up past every row and passes through each row's socket.

2 · One row's socket, wiring view

The socket is a TE MTA-100 5-pin feed-through housing. Its pins line up with the 5-pin header on the rightmost digit module: +12V · GND · A · B · ENUM. The housing is drawn face-on so all five wires are visible; in the rail it stands on its end, matching the header.

3 · How the row plugs in, seen from above

The printed row guide does the coarse alignment. The socket sits loose in a pocket with a funnel in front, so the header can steer it the last millimetre. The row stops against an end stop, not against the pins.

Open points

Row current

Drew measures a full row next; the 25 A, the PSU and the fuse sizes follow from that.

ENUM

Not settled. A wire through every row's pin 5 joins all rows' ENUM together. That fits one scheme and rules out the other.

Tapping the bus

Every row needs its own drop, fused or not, so the bus needs 21 taps. Soldering onto a stripped section is the standard way; cutting the bus at each row puts up to 25 A through every joint. Two ways to avoid taps altogether are in rail.md: a 22 AWG pair per row straight from fuse blocks in the bottom bay (leaning), or two drilled bus bars with ring terminals.

IDC is press-once

Re-pressing a wire into an MTA slot isn't recommended. Practise on spare housings before doing the 21 on the rail.
