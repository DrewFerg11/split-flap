Split-flap module board v0.1 · bench test · no adapter

Three modules, with a fourth board as the bridge

No RS-485 adapter. A spare board stands in for one: the ESP32 sends plain 3.3 V serial into the bridge board's J6 pads, and the bridge's own RS-485 chip (U2) puts it on the bus. The bridge sits at the start of the row, and the 12 V supply goes into its J1. Boards 1–3 plug in after it (J1 into J2). The bridge needs no motor and no address.

Before you switch on

  1. J6 pad 1 (3V3) stays unconnected: the board makes its own 3.3 V. Only pads 5, 6 and 7 go to the ESP32.
  2. TX goes to RX: the ESP's TX2 goes to pad 7 (the bridge's RX), and the bridge's pad 6 (TX) goes to the ESP's RX2.
  3. J1 pin 1 is the top pin (+12 V), as in pcb/module-board/design/images/bringup-meter-points.png.
  4. No JP_TERM shunts on any board. The bus is about 20 cm long, so it doesn't need termination, and with nothing on it the bus stays quiet when idle.

Tip: solder a 7-pin 2.54 mm male header into the bridge's J6. Then the ST-Link (pads 2–5) and the ESP32 (pads 5–7) both clip on with Dupont leads, with no rewiring when you reflash. Two leads can share pad 5 (GND) if you stack the Dupont connectors or splice them.

Bring-up order

  1. Bridge board and boards 2 and 3: unpowered shorts check, power-up, flash, LED (DESIGN-1 §14.1 step 2).
  2. Flash the bridge with the bridge build (firmware/module) (it copies bytes between J6 serial and RS-485). Flash boards 1–3 with firmware/module envs node1–node3.
  3. Bridge plus board 1 only: 1 ping, 1 home.
  4. Add boards 2 and 3: s (status of all), * home, * go 17.

Later, a MAX3485 adapter replaces the bridge (see bench-max3485.html); nothing else changes.
