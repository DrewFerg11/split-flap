Split-flap module board v0.1 · bench test

Three modules on one RS-485 bus

The ESP32 is powered over USB and talks to a 3.3 V RS-485 adapter. The adapter's A/B lines and the 12 V supply go into board 1's J1 header. Boards 2 and 3 plug straight into the board before them (J1 into J2), so power and bus pass down the row with no extra wiring. Each board drives its own motor and reads its own hall sensor.

Controller:

Any of the three works. Pins below and in the diagram follow this choice. Flash with pio run -t upload in firmware/controller.

Before you switch on

  1. Power the adapter's VCC from the ESP32's 3V3 pin, never from VIN/5V: at 5 V its RXD output would put 5 V on GPIO16.
  2. J1 pin 1 is the top pin (+12 V), as in pcb/module-board/design/images/bringup-meter-points.png. Getting pins 1 and 2 swapped is what Q1 protects against, but check anyway.
  3. ESP32 GND, adapter GND and the PSU's − must all be connected. RS-485 needs a shared ground.
  4. J1 pin 5 (ENUM) goes to an ESP GPIO, never to 12 V: the board's 1 k / 10 k would put ~11 V on the STM32.
  5. Shunt on JP_TERM on board 3 only. The adapter end may or may not have its own 120 Ω; on a short bench bus it works either way.

This is the 5-pin module (EN VCC RXD TXD GND on one side, A / B on the other). Go by the printed labels, not by position. Modules don't answer? Swap the TXD and RXD wires: some sellers label them backwards. Swapping is harmless.

Bring-up order

  1. Boards 2 and 3: unpowered shorts check, power-up, flash, LED. Same as board 1 (DESIGN-1 §14.1 step 2).
  2. Flash every board with the same node build (firmware/module); the controller addresses them over the ENUM chain.
  3. Board 1 alone on the adapter: 1 ping, 1 home.
  4. ENUM check (once the host drives the ENUM pin): s shows enum 1 on board 1 with the pin high, 0 with it low.
  5. Plug in boards 2 and 3, set the JP_TERM shunt on board 3, then s (status of all), * home, * go 17.
